What is a breast lift?

A breast lift, or mastopexy, raises breasts that have descended and gives them a firmer, more youthful shape.

Over time the skin and supporting tissue of the breast can stretch, so the breast sits lower and the nipple may point downwards. This is called ptosis. A breast lift removes the extra skin, reshapes the breast tissue and moves the nipple and areola to a higher, more natural position. Stretched areolae can be made smaller at the same time.

A lift changes the shape and position of the breast rather than its size. If you would also like more fullness, it can be combined with implants; if your breasts feel heavy, it can be combined with a reduction.

How drooping is assessed

  • Mild: the nipple sits level with, or just below, the crease under the breast.
  • Moderate: the nipple sits below the crease.
  • Advanced: the nipple sits well below the crease and points downwards.
  • Glandular: the nipple stays in place but the breast tissue has dropped below the crease.

The degree of drooping helps Dr Davide choose the right technique and scar pattern for you.

What a breast lift can address

  • Breasts that have dropped or flattened
  • Nipples that sit low or point downwards
  • Loose skin after pregnancy or weight loss
  • Loss of firmness after breastfeeding
  • Large or stretched areolae
  • One breast sitting lower than the other
  • Breasts that look empty despite enough volume

The benefits

  • A higher, firmer breast position
  • Better shape and projection
  • Nipples and areolae at a natural height
  • Improved symmetry between the breasts
  • Excess, stretched skin removed
  • Better proportion with the rest of your figure
  • Clothes and swimwear that fit more comfortably

Is a breast lift right for you?

A breast lift suits you if your breasts have dropped or changed shape but you are broadly happy with their size. You should be in good general health, a non-smoker or willing to stop before surgery, and at a stable weight.

If you are planning a pregnancy or expect to lose a lot of weight, it is usually best to wait, as both can change the result. If you would like noticeably fuller breasts, Dr Davide may suggest adding implants.

What the operation involves

A breast lift is carried out under general anaesthetic and usually takes two to three hours. The scar pattern depends on how much lift you need.

Around the areola

A circular scar around the edge of the areola.

For mild drooping, a small lift or reducing the size of the areola.

Vertical, or lollipop

Around the areola and straight down to the crease.

For moderate drooping, giving more reshaping and lift.

Anchor

Around the areola, down to the crease and along it.

For advanced drooping or a lot of loose skin.

During the operation, excess skin is removed, the breast tissue is reshaped to give a firmer structure, and the nipple and areola are moved to their new position. The incisions are closed and you wake up in a supportive surgical bra.

Recovery and aftercare

Most patients feel comfortable returning to everyday life within two weeks. Full recovery takes around six weeks.

  1. First few days

    Some swelling, bruising and tightness, eased with pain relief. You wear a supportive surgical bra day and night.

  2. Weeks 1 to 2

    Gentle walking is encouraged. Many people return to desk work and light activity, and dressings are checked at your first review.

  3. Weeks 3 to 6

    Keep wearing the supportive bra and avoid heavy lifting, strenuous exercise and sleeping on your front.

  4. After 6 weeks

    Most normal activity, including exercise, can resume. Swelling continues to settle and the shape refines.

  5. 3 to 6 months

    Your final shape appears as the tissues settle. Scars soften and fade, with follow-up for a year or more.

Risks and considerations

All surgery carries risks. Dr Davide explains how each applies to you at your consultation. Possible risks of a breast lift include:

  • Bleeding, infection or fluid collection
  • Slow wound healing or more noticeable scars
  • Differences in shape or nipple position between the breasts
  • Temporary or permanent changes in nipple sensation
  • Reduced blood supply to the nipple or skin
  • Some drooping returning over time, or a need for further surgery

Smoking, some medical conditions and big changes in weight can increase these risks.

Your safety comes first. You meet the anaesthetist before surgery and are followed up by Dr Davide personally for a year or more.
